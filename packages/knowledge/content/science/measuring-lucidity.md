Measuring lucid dreaming sounds simple: did you know you were dreaming, and how often? In practice, how the question is asked changes the answer. This article covers the scales the app uses and the traps the research has found.

## Frequency

The recommended frequency item is the eight-point Lucid Dream Frequency Scale: never; less than once a year; about once a year; about two to four times a year; about once a month; about two to three times a month; about once a week; several times a week. Its four-week test-retest reliability was r = .89 in 93 sport students [B49][B50]. For analysis it converts to lucid dreams per month (0, 0.042, 0.083, 0.25, 1.0, 2.5, 4.0, 8.0) [B46].

## Recall

The Mannheim Dream Questionnaire uses a seven-point recall scale from "never" to "almost every morning", converted to mornings per week (0, 0.125, 0.25, 0.625, 1.0, 3.5, 6.5) [B50][B46]. In its 2,929-person validation sample, lucid-dream frequency correlated r = .36 with dream recall [B50].

Here is the trap. Retrospective single questions underestimate recall relative to logbooks, and logbooks themselves raise recall; Aspy (2016) found the disparity largest in people whose recall improved during the logbook period [B48]. Schredl (2002) found that a diary increased recall in low and medium recallers but decreased it in high recallers [C30]. So the app tracks recall from the journal (dreams per morning) rather than from a one-off question, shows you that early journal weeks will inflate your apparent improvement, and keeps a separate retrospective item for comparison with published norms [B48].

## Per-dream lucidity

The LuCiD scale rates one dream on 28 items across eight factors: insight, control, thought, realism, memory, dissociation, negative emotion and positive emotion. Insight items such as "I was aware that I was dreaming" separate lucid from non-lucid dreams most strongly [B22]. The app uses the insight and control ideas as 1 to 5 sliders and offers the full scale only for export [B22]. The shorter Dream Lucidity Questionnaire rates awareness, control and access to waking memory on a 0 to 4 scale; its item count could not be confirmed from the original [B25].

## Dream quantity

Aspy's studies rate each recalled dream as fragmentary (1), partial (2), majority (4) or whole (8), summed per night [C33]. The app uses this as its recall clarity scale. The Lucidity Institute's rule is simpler and also kept: any fragment counts as one dream [C2].

## Nightmares and bad dreams

Zadra and Donderi separated nightmares (disturbing dreams that wake you) from bad dreams (disturbing dreams that do not). In 89 participants keeping four-week logs, log-based frequencies were significantly higher than retrospective estimates, and nightmare frequency correlated more with poorer well-being than bad-dream frequency [C18]. The app therefore logs "woke me up" as a flag and computes both frequencies from the journal rather than asking you to estimate them [C18].

## Emotion

How emotion is measured drives the result. Self-ratings produce more emotional dreams and more positive emotion; external raters reading the text find mostly negative emotion, because positive emotion is under-reported in narrative [C21]. In 552 home dreams, the majority were positive by self-rating but negative by external rating [C21a]. The app asks for your own emotion rating per dream and treats any text-derived emotion as a secondary signal [C21].

## Statistical honesty

Dream series are autocorrelated and most people will have a small number of dreams. Domhoff and Schneider apply multiple-comparison corrections and autocorrelation checks even to long series [C12a]. The app shows counts next to every percentage, suppresses comparisons under about 20 dreams, and prefers "nights with technique X: 8 lucid of 40" over bare rates [C12a].

## Moon phase and other curiosities

Schredl, Fulda and Reinhard analysed diaries from 196 participants and found no association between full moon and dream recall; Cordi and colleagues found no lunar effect on sleep in 1,265 volunteers over 2,097 laboratory nights [C36][C49]. If the app shows moon phase at all, it is labelled "for curiosity, no evidence".
