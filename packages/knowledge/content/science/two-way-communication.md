Eye signals showed that lucid dreamers can send a message out of a dream. In 2021 researchers showed that messages can also go in, and be answered.

## The study

Karen Konkoly, Ken Paller and co-authors in four independent laboratories, at Northwestern University in the United States, Osnabrueck University in Germany, the Paris Brain Institute in France and the Donders Institute in the Netherlands, published "Real-time dialogue between experimenters and dreamers during REM sleep" in Current Biology [A54].

Sleepers in signal-verified lucid REM were asked questions by spoken words, Morse-coded lights or touch, and answered with eye movements or facial-muscle contractions [B27]. Questions included simple arithmetic, yes-or-no questions and counting stimuli [A54].

## The results

Across 36 participants and 57 REM sessions, 15 sessions (26 percent) contained signal-verified lucidity. Of 158 questions posed, 29 (18.4 percent) were answered correctly, 5 incorrectly, 28 ambiguously and 96 without response [A54]. In 47 percent of signal-verified lucid episodes at least one question was answered correctly, giving 29 correct answers from six people [B27].

The control condition matters. In non-lucid REM trials there were 366 no-response trials, 11 ambiguous, 1 correct and 1 incorrect [B27]. Answers came from lucid dreamers, not by chance.

## What it shows

This is the strongest evidence that lucid dreamers can process external information and reason in real time while asleep [B27]. A sleeper who can hear "eight minus six" and signal "two" with the eyes is demonstrably perceiving, remembering, calculating and acting inside REM sleep.

It also shows the limits. Most questions got no response at all, and lucidity was present in only about a quarter of sessions even with experienced participants [A54]. Communication is possible, not easy.

## What followed

The same group then used the "in" channel for induction. In a laboratory morning nap, a sound and light cue was paired with a lucid mindset for 20 minutes before sleep, then replayed in REM; 50 percent of participants had a signal-verified lucid dream in a single nap, including three with no prior lucid dreams [B42]. In 2024 they ported the method to an Android app; lucid dreams rose from 0.74 to 2.11 per week in the first experiment, though cues produced measurable sleep disruption [B43]. The tools article covers this.

## What it does not show

In September 2024 the company REMspace issued press releases claiming the first "two-way communication" between two sleeping people in lucid dreams, followed by claims of controlling a car from a dream. These results have not been peer-reviewed, and Konkoly has publicly criticised the company for releasing "unfinished, undocumented projects inaccurately represented as breakthroughs" [A57]. The peer-reviewed finding is dialogue between a dreamer and an experimenter in a laboratory, with modest success rates. Anything beyond that should be labelled as unverified marketing [A57].
