Marie-Jean-Leon, Marquis d'Hervey de Saint-Denys (1822 to 1892), a French scholar of Chinese, is the first person known to have kept a sustained dream journal and used it deliberately to train dream awareness [A17]. He began recording his dreams at thirteen or fourteen [A17][C5].

## The book

In 1867 he published, anonymously, Les reves et les moyens de les diriger: observations pratiques, "Dreams and the ways to direct them". Alfred Maury identified him as the author in a footnote to the 1878 edition of his own book on sleep and dreams [A17].

The book argues from his own records that attention to dream recall, together with associative cues, allows the dreamer to become aware inside the dream and to steer it. He used the phrase "reve lucide" roughly fifty years before van Eeden, though in a looser sense [A18].

## What he found

A 1983 review describes the book as "more than twenty years of experimentation with his own lucid dreams", notes his "ability to recall an unbroken sequence of images no matter when he was awakened", and summarises his central observation: he traced "every image and transition to associatively linked waking memories", concluding that dreams are "essentially continuous with waking thought, although different in form" [C5a].

He also ran what we would now call cue experiments, using "seven or eight different fragrances as stimuli to evoke associated ideas in his dreams" [C5a]. The same idea, a scent during sleep as a reminder, was tested in a sleep laboratory in 2020 and judged unpromising, but Saint-Denys was there first [B45].

## Numbers that cannot be checked

Frequently repeated figures, 1,946 nights of recorded dreams and a first lucid dream on the 207th night, come from a single encyclopaedia entry and could not be checked against the French text [A17]. Claims that he filled "22 volumes" could likewise not be verified from any source consulted [C5]. This app does not state them as fact.

## Translations

An abridged English translation, Dreams and How to Guide Them, edited by Morton Schatzman, appeared in 1982; it is an abridgement of a 1964 French edition [A17][C5]. A complete translation by Carolus den Blanken and Eli Meijer appeared in 2016 [A17].

## Why he matters to a journal app

Saint-Denys and, later, van Eeden are the origin of the practice that a dreamer who keeps a long diary notices recurring features in their own dreams [C6]. The specific typology of recurring features used in this app, the four categories of dream signs, is LaBerge's from 1990, but the method of patient recording and review is Saint-Denys' from 1867 [C1][C5a].
