A date-ordered list of the events covered in the history section. Each entry cites its source so you can follow it up.

## Before 1900

- c. 350 BCE: Aristotle, On Dreams, notes "something in consciousness which declares that what then presents itself is but a dream" [A3].
- 415: Augustine, Letter 159, recounts the dream of Gennadius [A6].
- 11th century: Naropa's Six Yogas, including dream yoga, are transmitted to Tibet [A11].
- c. 1200: Ibn al-Arabi on controlling thoughts in dreams, as reported in later literature [A7].
- c. 1270: Aquinas, Summa Theologiae: a sleeper "may judge that what he sees is a dream" [A15].
- 1867: Hervey de Saint-Denys publishes Les reves et les moyens de les diriger anonymously [A17].

## 1900 to 1970

- 1913: Van Eeden, "A Study of Dreams", coins "lucid dream" and reports 352 cases from 1898 to 1912 [A19].
- 1921: Mary Arnold-Forster, Studies in Dreams [A22].
- 1935: Evans-Wentz's Tibetan Yoga and Secret Doctrines, the first scholarly English presentation of dream yoga [D35].
- 1939: Oliver Fox, Astral Projection [A23].
- 1948: Nathan Rapport, "Pleasant Dreams!" [A24].
- 1959: Tholey develops the reflection technique at Frankfurt [A25].
- 1968: Celia Green, Lucid Dreams [A28].

## 1970 to 2000

- 1972 to 1974: Ann Faraday, Dream Power; Patricia Garfield, Creative Dreaming [A29][A30].
- 12 April 1975: Hearne records Worsley's eye signals at Hull [A34].
- 13 January 1978: LaBerge's first signal-verified lucid dream at Stanford [A35].
- May 1978: Hearne's PhD thesis submitted at Liverpool [A36].
- 1980: LaBerge's PhD; "Lucid dreaming as a learnable skill" [A37][A42].
- 1981: LaBerge, Nagel, Dement and Zarcone in Perceptual and Motor Skills; Lucidity Letter begins [A38][A39].
- 1983: Tholey, "Techniques for inducing and manipulating lucid dreams"; the Association for the Study of Dreams is founded; Hearne's dream-machine patents [A26][A40][A34].
- 1985: LaBerge, Lucid Dreaming; Domhoff, The Mystique of Dreams, debunks the Senoi story [A42][A31].
- 1987: The Lucidity Institute is founded; Tholey and Utecht, Schoepferisch traeumen [A42][A27].
- 1988: Gackenbach and LaBerge edit Conscious Mind, Sleeping Brain [A41].
- 1990: LaBerge and Rheingold, Exploring the World of Lucid Dreaming [A42].
- 1993: The NovaDreamer is released [A42].
- 1995: LaBerge and Levitan validate DreamLight cues [B41].
- 1996: LD4all is founded [A58].

## 2000 to the present

- 2004: The Association for the Study of Dreams becomes the IASD [A40].
- 2009: Voss and colleagues report the 40 Hz "hybrid state" [A44].
- 2010: Erlacher and Schredl show motor practice in lucid dreams improves waking performance [A53].
- 2011: Schredl and Erlacher's representative sample finds 51 percent lifetime prevalence; SSILD is created on a Chinese forum [A60][A59].
- 2012: Dresler's fMRI case study; Stumbrys's systematic review; Voss's age study; the Remee Kickstarter [A47][A49][A61][A55].
- 2014: Voss and colleagues report tACS induction in Nature Neuroscience [A45].
- 2016: Saunders and colleagues' meta-analysis: 55 percent lifetime, 23 percent monthly [A21].
- 2017: Aspy's National Australian Lucid Dream Induction Study [A50].
- 2018: LaBerge, LaMarca and Baird's galantamine trial [A52].
- 2019: Baird, Mota-Rolim and Dresler's review [A48].
- 2020: Aspy's International Lucid Dream Induction Study; the tACS replication attempt finds no effect [A51][A46].
- 2021: Konkoly and colleagues, real-time dialogue during REM sleep [A54].
- 2024: Konkoly's group tests a cue-based app at home; REMspace press claims, unverified [B43][A57].
