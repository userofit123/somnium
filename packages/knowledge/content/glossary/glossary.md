One line per term. Where a term has a research definition, the source follows.

- ADA (all-day awareness): a community practice of noticing the small details of waking life until, as its author puts it, the dream itself becomes your dream sign [D20].
- Anchor: community word for whatever you attend to while falling asleep in a wake-initiated attempt: imagery, counting, a phrase, or the body [D1].
- Autosuggestion: a low-effort, relaxed suggestion to yourself that you will have a lucid dream, without willing it [D1].
- Bad dream: a disturbing dream that does not wake you, as distinct from a nightmare [C18].
- CAT (Cycle Adjustment Technique): Daniel Love's schedule-based method of waking 90 minutes early on alternating days [D24].
- Chaining: re-entering a dream repeatedly by lying still after each awakening; the community form of dream re-entry [D22].
- Continuity hypothesis: Domhoff's view that dream content is continuous with the dreamer's waking conceptions and concerns [C12].
- Critical state testing: LaBerge's name for reality testing with a genuine question, adapted from Tholey [D1].
- Day residue: material from the immediately preceding day appearing in a dream [C23].
- DEILD (Dream Exit Initiated Lucid Dream): staying completely still as a dream ends so that REM resumes and you re-enter aware [D22].
- DILD (dream-initiated lucid dream): a lucid dream that begins inside an ordinary dream, usually by noticing a dream sign [D1].
- Dream control: influencing the content or course of a dream; separate from, and rarer than, lucidity [B31].
- Dream incubation: choosing a problem or scene before sleep so that the dream addresses it [D27].
- Dream lag: the reappearance of waking material in dreams about five to seven days later [C24].
- Dream recall: remembering dreams on waking; the strongest predictor of lucid dreaming and the foundation of every programme [B33].
- Dream sign: a peculiar, recurring feature of your own dreams that can cue recognition of the dreaming state [C1].
- Dream yoga (milam): the Tibetan practice of recognising and working with the dream state, one of the Six Yogas of Naropa [A11].
- Dreamsign Awareness Scale: the Lucidity Institute's 0 to 4 rating of how aware you were of a dream sign in a dream [C2].
- DreamLight: LaBerge and Levitan's REM-detecting mask that flashed light cues, validated in 1995 [B41].
- EEG: recording of the brain's electrical activity from the scalp, used to identify sleep stages [B1].
- EOG (electro-oculogram): recording of eye movements, the channel through which lucid dreamers signal [B15].
- False awakening: a dream of having woken up [B8].
- FILD (Finger Induced Lucid Dream): tiny alternating finger movements at the edge of sleep, then a nose-pinch test [D21].
- Frequent lucid dreamer: someone with one or more lucid dreams a month, by the research convention [B51].
- Galantamine: a prescription cholinesterase inhibitor shown to increase lucid dreams after wake-back-to-bed; not recommended by this app [B52][B56].
- Hall and Van de Castle system: the standard method for coding dream content into characters, interactions, emotions and settings [C8].
- Hypnagogic: the state and imagery at sleep onset [B5].
- Hypnopompic: the state and imagery on waking [B5].
- Imagery rehearsal therapy: rewriting a nightmare while awake and rehearsing the new version; the better-evidenced nightmare treatment [B61].
- Klartraum: Tholey's German term for the lucid dream [A26].
- LRLR: the standard left-right-left-right eye signal that verifies lucidity in the laboratory [B15].
- LuCiD scale: the 28-item Lucidity and Consciousness in Dreams scale with eight factors, for rating a single dream [B22].
- Lucid dream: a dream in which you know, while it is still going on, that you are dreaming [A1].
- MADRE: the Mannheim Dream Questionnaire, the source of the standard frequency and recall scales and their norms [B50].
- MILD (Mnemonic Induction of Lucid Dreams): LaBerge's intention-and-visualisation technique practised on waking from a dream [D1].
- Nightmare: a disturbing dream that wakes you [C18].
- NovaDreamer: the consumer version of the DreamLight, sold from 1993 to about 2004 [A42].
- Polysomnography: simultaneous recording of brain activity, eye movements and muscle tone during sleep [B16].
- Prospective memory: remembering to do something later; the skill MILD depends on [D1].
- Reality test (reality check): a deliberate check of whether you are dreaming, paired with the question "Am I dreaming?" [D1].
- Recurrent dream: the same dream story repeated, as distinct from a recurring dream sign [C17].
- Reflection technique: Tholey's 1959 method of asking "Am I awake, or am I dreaming?" several times a day [D8].
- REM sleep: the sleep stage with rapid eye movements, a wake-like brain rhythm and muscle paralysis, in which most vivid dreaming and nearly all lucid dreaming occur [B1].
- REM rebound: the increase in REM sleep after it has been suppressed, as after stopping cannabis or alcohol [B9].
- Semi-lucid: a dream rated in the middle of an awareness scale, suspecting without concluding [C41a].
- Signal-verified lucid dream: a lucid dream confirmed by a pre-agreed eye signal during polysomnographically confirmed REM sleep [B15].
- Sleep paralysis: REM paralysis persisting into wakefulness, often with hallucinations; harmless and self-limiting [B6].
- Spinning: LaBerge's stabilisation method of spinning the dream body when the dream fades [D1].
- SSILD (Senses-Initiated Lucid Dream): cycling attention through sight, hearing and touch after a short night awakening [D19].
- Stabilisation: keeping a lucid dream going by engaging the senses and moving [D1].
- tACS: transcranial alternating current stimulation; the 40 Hz induction result has not replicated [B23][B24].
- Targeted lucidity reactivation: pairing a sound with a lucid mindset before sleep and replaying it in REM [B42].
- Typical Dreams Questionnaire: a 55-item checklist of common dream themes with population prevalence [C13].
- VILD (Visually Incubated Lucid Dream): rehearsing a short fixed scene containing a reality check so that it plays as a dream [D23].
- WBTB (wake-back-to-bed): sleeping five to six hours, getting up for a while, and returning to sleep with a technique [D4].
- WILD (wake-initiated lucid dream): a lucid dream entered directly from waking with unbroken awareness [D5].
