## Dry spells

Expect them. LaBerge's remedies are rest, renewed motivation, and switching to a lower-effort method when concentration is low [D1][D5]. "If you find you are losing sleep while struggling to have lucid dreams without result, let go of your efforts for a while. Relax and forget about lucid dreaming for a few days or a few weeks. Sometimes you will find that after you let go, lucid dreams will appear" [D1]. Go back to the basics that predict success: recall and the journal [B38][D20]. Love suggests refreshing the first stage of his method every few months [D24].

## Losing sleep

The cautionary literature's main worry is that induction methods fragment sleep [B69][B70]. Lucid dreams themselves did not lower sleep quality in the studies that measured it [B38]. If daytime sleepiness, mood or concentration suffer, cut WBTB to one or two nights a week, or drop to intention and reflection only for a week [D1]. SSILD's author lists losing sleep as its failure mode: sleep longer before the alarm, or stay up for less time [D19].

## False awakenings

Do a reality check on every awakening, after spinning, after SSILD, and before you get up for WBTB [D1][D5][D19]. False awakenings are common among lucid dreamers [B8].

## Trying too hard

Most bad experiences reported online came after failed or forced attempts [B76]. Do not force WILD, do not stare, and if an attempt fails, let it go. Sleep paralysis is harmless and ends on its own [B6][B7].

## Who should be careful

Talk to a clinician before practising if you have a psychotic or bipolar disorder [B72], a dissociative disorder [B71], an active sleep disorder [B69], or are in acute depression without support [B73]. If lucid dreams become distressing, or waking life starts to feel dreamlike, pause and talk to a professional [B71][B70].
