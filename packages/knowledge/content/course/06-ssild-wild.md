## What to do

SSILD comes after the same WBTB alarm [D19]. Lie down, close your eyes and move your attention in turn through sight (the darkness behind your eyelids, without straining), hearing (any sound, inside or out) and touch (weight, tingling, the blanket). Do a few quick cycles, then a few slow ones, then let yourself sleep [D19]. Its author's advice: "expect NOTHING" [D19]. Do a reality check on every awakening afterwards: false awakenings are common [D19].

WILD keeps awareness through sleep onset. Relax completely, then watch the images that appear behind your eyes "as delicately as possible", without holding on [D1]. When they become a moving scene, let yourself be carried into it passively; trying to enter makes it vanish [D1]. Try it after WBTB or in a morning nap, not at bedtime, when the first REM period is short and far away [D19].

## What to expect

On the way in you may feel unable to move, weight on the chest, buzzing or vibration. This is sleep paralysis: the REM state you are in every night, with the mind awake. "Sleep paralysis can be quite terrifying, but it is harmless" [D1]. It ends on its own; relax and breathe, or wiggle a finger or toe [B6][B7].

## Why

SSILD has one large field study: after WBTB it gave lucid dreams on 16.9 percent of nights, as many as MILD [B38]. With one study behind it, the reviews call it promising but unconfirmed [B35]. WILD is documented in the lab, but no controlled trial has tested it as a technique [B15]. In one lab, most lucid dreams after WBTB began from a dream anyway [D13]. Alternate them with MILD rather than replacing it [D12].

## Today's practice

- Planned one night for SSILD after WBTB, keeping MILD on other nights
- Read what sleep paralysis feels like, so it does not surprise you
- Did a reality check on waking this morning

## Ready to move on

After trying SSILD or WILD on 4 nights, or after 4 weeks. You can move on sooner whenever you like.
