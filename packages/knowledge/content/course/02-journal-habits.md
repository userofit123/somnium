## What to do

Give each dream a short title and its date, and write what you saw, heard and felt. LaBerge asks for how the images "look and sound and smell" and "the way you felt in the dream - emotional reactions are important clues", and for anything unusual [C1]. Record fragments too, "no matter how unimportant" [C1]. Writing in the present tense keeps you inside the dream as you write; mark the people, places and things in it as elements, so that Signs can count them.

Then do the dream sign inventory. Once you have about a dozen dreams, mark the dream signs in each: the odd things that could have told you it was a dream [C1]. Sort them into LaBerge's four categories: inner awareness (odd thoughts or feelings), action (things you or others do that could not happen), form (people or objects that are the wrong shape) and context (the wrong place or time) [C1]. Count which category comes up most often: that is your target [C1].

## Why

Dream signs are how lucidity usually starts. The cataloguing exercise is the first step of LaBerge's programme, and MILD later uses a sign from your own dreams in its visualisation [C1][D1]. The Lucidity Institute's course has you read your last ten dreams, mark every sign and pick target categories, because noticing the strangeness of dreams goes with reaching lucidity [C2][D5].
