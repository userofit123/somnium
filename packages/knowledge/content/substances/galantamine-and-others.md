This app does not recommend any drug or supplement for lucid dreaming. The evidence is summarised here so that you meet accurate information rather than forum lore [B54]. Nothing on this page is medical advice.

## Galantamine

Galantamine is a prescription acetylcholinesterase inhibitor, licensed for Alzheimer's disease, that increases cholinergic tone, which drives REM sleep [B52].

The evidence: LaBerge, LaMarca and Baird (2018) ran a double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover in 121 experienced, high-recall participants. On three nights, in counterbalanced order, they woke after about 4.5 hours, took 0, 4 or 8 mg, stayed up about 30 minutes, then practised MILD. Lucid dreams were reported by 14 percent on placebo, 27 percent on 4 mg and 42 percent on 8 mg; 57 percent had a lucid dream on at least one active night [B52]. A smaller eight-night home study (35 people) found 40 percent lucid with wake-back-to-bed plus galantamine versus 9 percent with placebo [B53][B54]. Because the placebo arm already included wake-back-to-bed and MILD, the drug effect sits on top of the best cognitive protocol, which is why it looks large [B52].

The side effects: mild side effects occurred in 12 percent on active doses versus 3 percent on placebo: gastrointestinal upset, nausea, difficulty returning to sleep, insomnia, next-day fatigue [B52]. Asthma, beta-blocker use, cardiac arrhythmia and severe mental illness were exclusion criteria, and sleep was not objectively measured [B52].

The contraindications: prescribing information warns of effects on cardiac conduction (slow heart rate, heart block, fainting, dose-related), gastrointestinal effects, and advises monitoring in severe asthma or COPD, seizure disorders and risk of gastrointestinal bleeding [B56]. The researchers themselves warn against unsupervised use [D37].

The app's position: galantamine is a prescription drug with cardiac and respiratory cautions, and the app does not recommend it [B52][B56].

## Other cholinergics: choline, alpha-GPC, huperzine A

Choline and alpha-GPC are sold as "natural galantamine". The only controlled test of alpha-GPC (33 participants, no mental training) found no effect: 6 lucid dreams split evenly between active and placebo nights [B54]. Huperzine A appears only in a patent application [B54].

## Nicotine

Nicotine patches worn overnight are promoted in some books and forums. There is no study of nicotine and lucid dreaming [B54]. What is known: 24-hour patches in smokers increased wake time and micro-arousals, reduced REM and produced more vivid dream reports [B12]; in 12 non-smokers, 7 and 14 mg patches caused early-morning awakening and dose-dependent REM loss, with rebound on the recovery night [B11]; patch labels advise removal at bedtime if vivid dreams or sleep disturbance occur [B11]. Nicotine is addictive and non-smokers tolerate it poorly. This practice is unsafe and unsupported [B11][B12].

## Vitamin B6

Aspy, Madden and Delfabbro randomised 100 Australians to 240 mg pyridoxine, a B-complex, or placebo for five nights. B6 increased the amount of dream content recalled, about 64 percent more, without changing vividness, bizarreness, colour or sleep; the B-complex group reported worse sleep quality and more tiredness. Lucidity was not increased [B55]. Long-term high-dose B6 carries a known risk of peripheral neuropathy [B55].

## Melatonin

There are no studies of melatonin as a lucid-dream inducer [B54]. In two small trials in patients with pathologically reduced REM, 3 mg for four weeks raised REM from about 14 to about 18 percent [B13]. Its effect on healthy sleepers' REM is small and inconsistent, and vivid-dream reports are anecdotal [B13][B54].

## Cannabis

THC suppresses REM eye-movement density and, to a lesser degree, REM duration; withdrawal produces a rebound above baseline [B10]. Heavy users abstaining show lower total sleep and slow-wave sleep and shortened REM latency on night 2 [B14]. The vivid dreams after stopping cannabis are REM rebound, not a technique, and they probably fade within weeks, though no source cited here measures how long.

## Alcohol and other drugs

Many drugs suppress REM and produce a later rebound. LaBerge: "If you go to sleep drunk, you may sleep quite soundly but dream little, until five or six hours into sleep", after which dreaming is more intense and often unpleasant [D1].

## "Dream herbs" and medicines

Mugwort, Calea ternifolia, Silene capensis, 5-HTP and similar herbs have ethnographic reports or single old experiments on recall and vividness, and no lucid-dreaming trials [B54]. Pregabalin, L-dopa and dopamine agonists have case reports of vivid or lucid dreams and nightmares as adverse effects [B54].

## The safety rule

Do not use nicotine, prescription cholinesterase inhibitors or high-dose supplements for dreaming [B52][B56]. If you are under 18, pregnant, or have cardiac, respiratory or seizure conditions, no substance should be contemplated at all [B52][B56]. The techniques with the best evidence cost nothing and carry no pharmacological risk; this app teaches those.
