Some dream themes are so common that almost everyone has had them. The Typical Dreams Questionnaire measures how common, and the app uses its figures to tell you whether a theme in your journal is ordinary or rare.

## The questionnaire

The TDQ is a 55-item checklist built from a 1958 Japanese and American comparison, with items modified and added; respondents tick each theme they have ever dreamed [C13][C47]. Nielsen, Zadra and colleagues gave it to 1,181 first-year students in three Canadian cities [C13]. Schredl and colleagues gave the same items to 444 German participants and found "most of the 55 dream themes occurred at least once in most of the participants' lifetimes", with a very high rank-order correlation with the Canadian data, so the relative frequencies are stable across samples [C14]. A 2025 internet-sample replication cites the same top figures [C16].

## Further down the list

Discovering a new room at home (32.3 percent), losing control of a vehicle (32.0), being half awake and paralysed in bed (27.2), having magical powers other than flying (24.9), teeth falling out (18.8), and being an object such as a tree or rock (3.5) are among the remaining 35 items [C13]. Four themes exceed 60 percent in both sexes: being chased, falling, school, and sexual experiences [C13a].

## Sex differences

The paper reports significant differences for sexual experiences, school, a living person as dead, flying, failing an exam, being frozen with fright, magical powers, superior knowledge, killing someone and extra-terrestrials, among others [C13].

## Recurrent dreams

A recurrent dream is the same story repeated, as distinct from a recurring dream sign, the same element across different dreams; the app records both [C17]. Secondary sources put the share of adults with at least one recurrent dream series at roughly 60 to 80 percent; the exact wording sometimes quoted could not be traced to a primary source. Zadra's 1996 study of 110 adults found 77.3 percent of recurrent dreams were disturbing, with "escapes and pursuits" the top theme at 25.9 percent, followed by accidents and misfortunes (19.7) and aggression and violence (19.0) [C17].

## How the app uses this

The 55 items are the app's seed vocabulary for typical themes. When a theme appears in your journal, the app can show its population prevalence, so that "being chased" reads as the commonest dream there is and "being an object" as genuinely unusual [C13]. Many of these themes are also dream signs: flying, a dead person alive, being a child again and sudden paralysis are oddities you can learn to notice [C1].
