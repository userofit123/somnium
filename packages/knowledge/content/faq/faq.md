## Is lucid dreaming real?

Yes. Since 1975, sleepers have made pre-agreed eye movements while instruments showed they were in REM sleep, and in 2021 lucid dreamers answered arithmetic questions from inside a dream [A34][A38][A54].

## How common is it?

About 55 percent of people have had at least one lucid dream and about 23 percent have them monthly or more [A21].

## Can anyone learn it?

Most people can increase their rate. In the largest home study, ordinary volunteers went from lucid dreams on 5.3 percent of nights to 15.8 percent after one week of MILD or SSILD with wake-back-to-bed [B38]. Some people respond much more than others, and dream recall is the best predictor [B38].

## How long does it take?

In the two large home studies, one week of practice roughly doubled or tripled the rate [B37][B38]. Tholey's figure for his method was a median of four to five weeks to the first lucid dream, and that is his own report [D1].

## Which technique should I start with?

Dream recall first, then dream signs and reality testing as habits, then MILD after a night awakening. MILD with wake-back-to-bed is the best-supported method [B35][B38].

## Do reality checks work?

Not by themselves, in the studies that tested them over a week. They add nothing to MILD and daily counts did not predict lucid dreams [B37][B38]. They are a useful habit for catching false awakenings and for building the questioning attitude that MILD relies on [B35][D8].

## What is the best reality check?

LaBerge's: read some text, look away, read it again; in dreams it changes [D1][D3]. Tholey's: try to remember what happened in the last few minutes, and turn on a light after any apparent awakening [C4].

## Does it harm sleep?

Lucid dreams themselves do not seem to; the induction studies found no deterioration in sleep quality and slightly better ratings on lucid nights [B38][B68]. Interrupting sleep to practise can, which is why the app limits wake-back-to-bed to a few nights a week [B69].

## Is it dangerous?

For most people, no. People with psychotic, bipolar or dissociative conditions, active sleep disorders, or acute depression, grief or PTSD should get advice first [B72][B71][B69][B60]. Stop if lucid dreams become distressing or waking life starts to feel dreamlike [B71][B70].

## What is sleep paralysis, and is it harmful?

REM paralysis persisting into wakefulness, often with hallucinations. It is common, unpleasant and physiologically harmless; relax and it passes [B6][D1].

## What is a false awakening?

A dream of having woken up. Test your state on every awakening; false awakenings often turn into lucid dreams, though the figures behind this come from a self-selected group of habitual lucid dreamers [B8].

## Can I control my dreams?

Sometimes, and it is a separate skill from awareness: 77 percent of a Brazilian sample had been lucid but only 29 percent reported control [B31]. Start small; expectation shapes what happens [D1].

## Why do I wake up as soon as I become lucid?

Excitement. "Novice lucid dreamers often wake up the moment they become lucid." Keep calm, engage the senses, move, and spin when the dream fades [D1][D5].

## Should I take galantamine or a supplement?

This app does not recommend any substance. Galantamine increased lucid dreams in one large trial, but it is a prescription drug with cardiac and respiratory cautions; other supplements have no evidence or negative evidence [B52][B56][B54].

## Does vitamin B6 help?

It increased the amount of dream content recalled in one trial but did not increase lucidity, and high doses carry a risk of nerve damage [B55].

## Do dream masks or 40 Hz devices work?

The one validated mask went out of production in 2004; the 40 Hz stimulation result did not replicate; portable devices are of unproven reliability [B41][B24][A56].

## Do apps work?

One peer-reviewed study of a sound-cue app found an increase from 0.74 to 2.11 lucid dreams a week, with some sleep disruption. No other app claim has been tested [B43].

## Does meditation help?

Long-term meditators have more lucid dreams, but an eight-week mindfulness course did not increase them [B46].

## Can lucid dreaming treat nightmares?

It is a promising adjunct with thin evidence; imagery rehearsal therapy, done awake, is better evidenced, and several responders in the trials never became lucid [B57][B61][B63].

## Can I practise skills in a lucid dream?

Three small studies found improvements in coin tossing, finger tapping and darts after lucid practice; the samples who managed it were tiny [B64][B65][B66].

## What are dream signs?

The peculiar, recurring features of your own dreams, in four categories: inner awareness, action, form and context [C1].

## What is the most common dream?

Being chased: 81.5 percent of students had dreamed it, followed by sexual experiences, falling and school [C13].

## How do I remember more dreams?

Sleep later in the morning, set the intention, stay still on waking and ask "What was I dreaming?", and write at once [C1][C2].

## Is the Senoi story true?

No. Domhoff's investigation concluded that Kilton Stewart misattributed his own ideas to the Senoi [A31].

## What about Castaneda's "look at your hands"?

Don Juan is generally regarded as fabricated, but looking at your hands may still work as a memory cue and a stabiliser [A33][D1].
