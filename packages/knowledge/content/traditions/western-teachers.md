Several Western writers teach lucid dreaming together with contemplative practice. This article records only what RESEARCH.md could verify from their books or their own published descriptions, and says where it could not.

## B. Alan Wallace

Dreaming Yourself Awake (2012) presents shamatha, attentional stability through mindfulness of breathing, settling the mind in its natural state and awareness of awareness, as the foundation; then Western techniques (affirmations, planning and imagining outcomes, the reflective attitude, dream journals); then the dream yoga practices of "emanation and transformation" and the illusory nature of experience [D31]. A review by Stumbrys calls it "a nice introduction into both topics" [D31].

## Charlie Morley

Dreams of Awakening: Lucid Dreaming and Mindfulness of Dream and Sleep (2013; revised 2024) is structured as Ground, Path and Germination. Its second part gives "dozens of lucid dreaming techniques and dream yoga exercises" including dream recall, dream signs, reality checks, MILD, wake-back-to-bed and the "hypnagogic affirmation": falling asleep mentally reciting "When I dream, I know that I'm dreaming" [D32].

"Mindfulness of Dream and Sleep" is a programme Morley developed with the mindfulness teacher Rob Nairn, later adapted for military veterans with trauma-affected sleep. Its listed components are normalising trauma-affected sleep, hypnagogic mindfulness (yoga nidra), de-stigmatising nightmares, coherent breathing and lucid dreaming [D32]. His public weekend workshops cover basics, dream planning, guided meditations and optional scheduled lucid-dream sessions on the Saturday night [D32].

Not verified: the names "Weird technique" and "Columbo method" sometimes attributed to him [D32].

## Andrew Holecek

Dream Yoga: Illuminating Your Life Through Lucid Dreaming and the Tibetan Yogas of Sleep (2016), with a foreword by LaBerge, has chapters on sleep cycles, Western induction techniques, Eastern induction techniques, mindfulness, the "lion's gaze", illusory form practice, the first and higher stages of dream yoga, obstacles, sleep yoga and bardo yoga [D33]. His summary of the dream yoga stages as "recognising, transforming, multiplying, dissolving" is a paraphrase of the traditional lists [D30].

## Robert Waggoner

Lucid Dreaming: Gateway to the Inner Self (2009) proposes five stages of lucid development and recommends ignoring dream objects and figures to address questions or requests to "the awareness behind the dream". His frequently quoted position is that the lucid dreamer directs the dream rather than controlling it [D26]. Waggoner reports Castaneda's "look at your hands" as the route to his own early lucid dreams [D25][D26].

Not verified: the exact wording of the five stages, the sailor-and-wind metaphor, and a specific 0 to 5 numeric scale of lucidity sometimes attributed to him; quote from the book [D26][C41].

## Daniel Love

Are You Dreaming? Exploring Lucid Dreams (2013) moves from the nature of dreams and sleep through reality checks, dream diaries, MILD, WILD, his own Cycle Adjustment Technique and a "dream peg" memory system, to chapters on staying lucid and what to do when lucid [D24]. CAT, which he first circulated in 2004, has its own article [D24].

Not verified: his specific WILD instructions; only CAT was reproduced from his text [D24].

## Thomas Yuschak

Advanced Lucid Dreaming: The Power of Supplements (2006) is the source of the supplement-based induction approach, galantamine with wake-back-to-bed, later tested by LaBerge, LaMarca and Baird, who found a dose-dependent effect and warn against unsupervised use [D34][D37]. The specific WILD or "anchor" wording attributed to Yuschak could not be verified [D34]. This app does not recommend any substance; the substances article explains why [B52][B56].

## Patricia Garfield and Ann Faraday

Garfield's Creative Dreaming (1974) brought dream control, including lucidity, to a wide audience; Faraday's Dream Power (1972) and The Dream Game (1974) popularised the dream diary as a self-help tool [A29][A30]. Garfield's book also repeated the Senoi story, which has since been debunked [A31]. Of her own advice, only interview remarks on recall were retrievable: recall is a learned skill, and if you wake with nothing, "gently roll into the other sleep position and lie there for a moment" [C42].

## How to read this

Where a teacher's method matches a tested technique, the technique article gives the evidence. Where a teacher's claims go beyond what was verified, this app says so rather than filling the gap.
