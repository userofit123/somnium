Hervey de Saint-Denys began recording his dreams daily in his early teens and published his findings anonymously in Paris in 1867 as Les reves et les moyens de les diriger [A17][C5]. The English edition of 1982, Dreams and How to Guide Them, is an abridgement [C5].

## Twenty years of experiment

Judith Malamud's 1983 review describes the book as "more than twenty years of experimentation with his own lucid dreams" [C5a]. She notes his "ability to recall an unbroken sequence of images no matter when he was awakened", a skill he attributed to long practice rather than to talent [C5a].

## What he concluded

His central observation was that he could trace "every image and transition to associatively linked waking memories", from which he concluded that dreams are "essentially continuous with waking thought, although different in form" [C5a]. More than a century later, Domhoff's continuity hypothesis, which holds that dream content is continuous with the dreamer's waking conceptions and concerns, makes a similar claim on the basis of thousands of reports [C12].

## Scents as cues

He ran association experiments, using "seven or eight different fragrances as stimuli to evoke associated ideas in his dreams" [C5a]. The idea that a stimulus during sleep can be linked beforehand to a dream content, and so become a reminder, is the principle behind the cue devices of the 1980s and the sound-cue studies of the 2020s [B41][B42]. A 2020 laboratory test of scent cues specifically found them unpromising, but the experiment Saint-Denys ran at home was the same experiment [B45].

## Directing the dream

The book's title promises the means to direct dreams. His method, as the historical overview describes it, was attention to recall together with associative cues, which allowed him to become aware inside the dream and to steer it. He used the phrase "reve lucide" roughly fifty years before van Eeden, though in a looser sense [A18].

## Numbers to leave out

Figures that circulate widely, 1,946 nights of recorded dreams, a first lucid dream on the 207th night, and "22 volumes" of journals, come from a single encyclopaedia entry or from no traceable source and could not be checked against the French text [A17][C5]. This app does not repeat them as fact.

## Why his example matters

Saint-Denys is the proof of concept for this app: a person who keeps a dream journal for long enough notices the recurring features of their own dreams and can learn to recognise them from inside [C6]. The four-category taxonomy of dream signs came later, from LaBerge, but the practice of patient recording came from a French sinologist with a notebook [C1][C5a].
