Several of the techniques in this app did not come from laboratories or books but from online forums. One of them has since been tested and matched MILD. The others rest on their authors' reports.

## The forums

LD4all was announced by its founder pasQuale on 25 May 1996 in the Usenet group alt.dreams.lucid and added a discussion forum in March 1999; DreamViews dates from around 2003; the subreddit r/LucidDreaming was created in 2009 and passed 100,000 subscribers by 2014, though the last two dates rest on weaker sources [A58].

## SSILD: Cosmic Iron, 2011

SSILD was created in 2011 by a user known as Cosmic Iron on a Chinese lucid-dreaming forum, under a title he translates as "A Very Mysterious Technique", refined collaboratively, and posted in English on LD4all and DreamViews about a year later; the official tutorial 2.0 is dated 24 January 2013 [A59][D19]. A specific Chinese forum name appearing in some accounts could not be confirmed [A58].

In his own words, it is "a hybrid", neither WILD nor DILD; the repeated sensory focus "conditions the mind and body into a subtle state that is optimized for lucid dreams to occur naturally". He is candid that in 2011 "nobody knew why it worked", and his advice is to "expect NOTHING at all will happen" [D19]. In 2020 it became a peer-reviewed arm of the International Lucid Dream Induction Study, where it produced lucid dreams on 16.9 percent of nights, equal to MILD [A51][D12]. It is the only forum-born technique with a controlled field study behind it [D12].

## FILD: Hargarts, 2004

Posted on DreamViews on 16 July 2004 with "original directions from LD4ALL" appended, so first described on LD4all; previously called HILD after its creator [D21]. The author's key instruction: press the imaginary keys so lightly that "you should basically feel your muscles contract, but your fingers should hardly move", and "do not count your finger movements or talk to yourself in your head" [D21]. No study exists [D21].

## ADA: KingYoshi, 2011

"All Day Awareness, A DILD Tutorial" was posted on DreamViews on 28 March 2011; the author credits the concept to the member Naiya and says he coined the term [D20]. His report: 107 dream-initiated lucid dreams logged on the site, about 22 a month, 90 percent from awareness rather than a check, and the summary line that with practice "the dream itself becomes your dream sign" [D20]. He added, after reader feedback, "Be sure not to overwork yourself. If you start feeling mentally exhausted, take a day off" [D20].

## DEILD: Klace, around 2007

The practice of lying still to re-enter a dream is in LaBerge's book, quoting Alan Worsley [D1]. The acronym and the word "chaining" come from DreamViews; a post by Klace, reproduced on LD4all on 5 March 2007, presents DEILD as "a substitute for WILD" and claims a "100% success rate if done correctly", which replies in the thread disputed [D22].

## VILD: Peter Harrison

Posted as Pedro on LD4all in "The BIG VILD topic", with the author's full description archived on Wikibooks [D23]. Harrison claimed "100% success rate with me so far", disputed in the thread; there is no study [D23].

## CAT: Daniel Love, 2004

The Cycle Adjustment Technique is marked "(c) 2004 Daniel Love", circulated on LD4all and printed in full in his 2013 book. Love himself states that it "has not been researched widely enough for a definitive success rate" [D24].

## How to weigh them

A forum technique can be excellent; SSILD proved it. But a thread full of success reports is not a trial: the people who failed mostly did not post, and the author's own rate is one person's experience. The app labels each of these as anecdotal (community) except SSILD, and quotes the authors' own cautions alongside their claims [D19][D20][D21].
