A lucid dream is a dream in which the dreamer knows, while the dream is still going on, that he or she is dreaming [A1][A2]. That is the whole definition. You do not have to control the dream, fly, or remember your waking life in detail. You only have to notice, from inside the dream, that it is a dream.

The word "lucid" for this state is barely a century old. The Dutch psychiatrist Frederik van Eeden coined "lucid dream" in a paper read to the Society for Psychical Research in 1913 [A19]. Reports of the experience itself are far older: Aristotle wrote that "often, when one is asleep, there is something in consciousness which declares that what then presents itself is but a dream" [A3].

## Awareness and control are different things

People often assume a lucid dream means a dream you can steer. Researchers keep the two apart. The standard questionnaire definition says that in a lucid dream "one is aware that one is dreaming during the dream", and that this makes it possible to wake up deliberately, to influence the action actively, or simply to observe the dream passively [B50]. Control is optional.

The gap between the two is large. In a Brazilian online survey of 3,427 people, 77 percent had experienced lucidity but only 29 percent reported dream control [B31]. Among children and teenagers who had lucid dreams, only 37 percent said they could manipulate the dream [A61]. Many lucid dreams are simply watched with awareness.

## Two ways in

Lucid dreams are usually sorted by how they begin [D1][D5]:

- A dream-initiated lucid dream (DILD) starts inside an ordinary dream. Something odd catches your attention, or a habit of questioning your state carries over into sleep, and you realise you are dreaming [D1].
- A wake-initiated lucid dream (WILD) is entered directly from the waking state, with awareness kept through the transition into sleep. These are most common in the late morning and in naps, when the drive to enter REM sleep is strongest [D5].

Most of the techniques in this app aim at the first kind. The second is harder and is the route most likely to pass through sleep paralysis and the strange imagery of falling asleep [B15].

## It is a real, measurable state

For a long time lucid dreaming was treated as a curiosity or doubted altogether. That changed in the late 1970s, when sleepers in laboratories in Hull and Stanford made pre-agreed eye movements while instruments showed they were in REM sleep [A34][A38]. Because the eyes are not paralysed during REM, a lucid dreamer can send a signal out of the dream [B1]. That method underpins everything the science section of this app describes.

## How common it is

Pooling 34 studies and more than 24,000 people, a 2016 meta-analysis estimated that about 55 percent of people have had at least one lucid dream and about 23 percent have them once a month or more [A21]. So the experience is ordinary; what is unusual is having it reliably and on purpose. That is what training is for.

## What this app means by the word

Throughout this app, "lucid" means aware that you are dreaming. When an article talks about control, stabilisation or steering a dream, it says so. The journal asks you whether a dream was lucid, and separately how much awareness and control you had, because the research treats these as separate questions [B22].
