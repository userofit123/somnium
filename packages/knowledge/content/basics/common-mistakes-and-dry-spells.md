Most plateaus in lucid dreaming have a small number of causes. The research and teaching literature agrees on them closely.

## Trying too hard and losing sleep

LaBerge wrote that one of the greatest stumbling blocks in learning almost any skill is trying too hard, and that if you are losing sleep while struggling to have lucid dreams, you should let go for a few days or weeks, since lucid dreams sometimes appear after you stop [D1]. The author of SSILD lists losing sleep and insomnia as the technique's failure modes and prescribes less time asleep before the alarm or less time awake [D19]. KingYoshi advises taking a day off when you are mentally exhausted [D20].

## Forcing WILD

Tholey held that actively wanting the experience dissolves the scenery; you have to be carried in passively [D1]. SSILD says to expect nothing and not to strain the eyes [D19]. FILD says not to count, narrate or lie there for 20 minutes [D21]. Erlacher's laboratory data suggest that most successes after wake-back-to-bed are dream-initiated anyway [D13].

## Mechanical reality checks

LaBerge, Tholey, Aspy and KingYoshi all warn that an automatic "obviously I am awake" trains the same response inside the dream [D1][D8][D11][D20]. A week of daytime checks gave no benefit in the international study [D12]. The remedy is fewer, planned checks in dreamlike situations, with a real question each time.

## Poor recall and an irregular journal

Every programme makes recall the gate, and recall predicted success [D5][D12]. Write something even when you recall nothing [D20].

## Waking up from excitement

If excitement wakes you, keep calm and engage with the dream. Spin when the vision fades, and "play dead" if you do wake [D1][D5].

## Expectation and control

LaBerge could not spin anywhere but his own bedroom, which shows how accidental expectations fix outcomes; start scene changes small [D1]. Tholey noted that beginners cannot easily produce experiences that contradict waking life, and that aggression breeds avengers, so use dialogue and looking [D8][D10].

## False awakenings

Test on every awakening. SSILD users have run cycles inside dreams and lost sleep [D19].

## Dry spells

The literature treats them as expected. The usual remedies are rest, renewed motivation, and a lower-effort method such as autosuggestion when concentration is low [D1][D5]. Love's CAT prescribes refreshing its first stage every few months [D24]. Community reports describe dry spells of a month or two ending with a return to daytime awareness plus MILD, but those reports are anecdotal [D20]. The Lucidity Institute course turns a plateau into a review of what came before each failure, using a record of lost lucidity [D5].

## Escaping a nightmare by waking

This produces false awakenings and leaves the fear unaddressed. The advice is to face the figure, talk to it and reconcile [D1][D10].
