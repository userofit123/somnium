"Lucid" is a yes-or-no word, but lucidity comes in degrees. Research uses two kinds of scale: one for how often you have lucid dreams, and one for how lucid a particular dream was. This app uses both, so it helps to know where they come from.

## The definition behind the scales

Questionnaires give the respondent a definition first: "In a lucid dream, one is aware that one is dreaming during the dream. Thus it is possible to wake up deliberately, or to influence the action of the dream actively, or to observe the course of the dream passively" [B50]. Snyder and Gackenbach argued in 1988 that vague definitions distort prevalence estimates, and set the convention that "frequent lucid dreamers" have at least one lucid dream a month [B51][B15].

## How often: the Lucid Dream Frequency Scale

The standard frequency item is an eight-point scale: 0 never, 1 less than once a year, 2 about once a year, 3 about two to four times a year, 4 about once a month, 5 about two to three times a month, 6 about once a week, 7 several times a week. In 93 sport students retested after four weeks, test-retest reliability was r = .89 [B49][B50]. For analysis it converts to lucid dreams per month: 0, 0.042, 0.083, 0.25, 1.0, 2.5, 4.0 and 8.0 [B46].

The Mannheim Dream Questionnaire (MADRE) packages this item with a seven-point dream recall scale (never, less than once a month, about once a month, two to three times a month, about once a week, several times a week, almost every morning), which converts to mornings per week: 0, 0.125, 0.25, 0.625, 1.0, 3.5 and 6.5 [B50][B46]. MADRE is free to reuse and gives the best comparable norms [B50].

## How lucid: the LuCiD scale

For rating a single dream, Voss and colleagues built the Lucidity and Consciousness in Dreams scale: 28 items rated 0 to 5, loading on eight factors: insight, control, thought, realism, memory, dissociation, negative emotion and positive emotion. Insight items such as "I was aware that I was dreaming" separate lucid from non-lucid dreams most strongly; realism and negative emotion did not differentiate them [B22][C35]. The full scale is too long for daily use, but its insight and control subscales make a sound per-dream lucidity grade [B22].

A shorter Dream Lucidity Questionnaire (DLQ) rates awareness of dreaming, control over content and access to waking memory on a 0 to 4 scale; sources describe it as 12 or 13 items, and the exact count could not be confirmed from the original [B25].

## The dreamsign awareness ladder

The Lucidity Institute's course rates how aware you were of each dream sign on a 0 to 4 scale: 0, you only noticed it was odd after waking; 1, you noticed it was odd during the dream but did not try to explain it; 2, you noticed and tried to explain it but did not become lucid; 3, you noticed and realised you were dreaming; 4, you were already lucid when the sign occurred [C2]. This is the only LaBerge-group rating scale found in primary text, and it works per sign rather than per dream [C2].

## Semi-lucid dreams

Recent research using a simple 0 to 4 awareness rating ("not at all" to "very much") treats the middle values as "semi-lucid" [C41a]. That matches the common experience of suspecting a dream without quite concluding it.

## What is not standard

Community "level of lucidity" ladders (1 to 5, "low, medium, high", "layers") exist on forums but are explicitly non-standard [C41a]. Robert Waggoner discusses levels and five stages of lucid development, but a specific numeric scale attributed to him could not be verified from any retrievable text [C41].

## What the app records

Following the research, each dream gets: lucid yes or no; a dreamsign awareness rating of 0 to 4 on each sign; and 1 to 5 sliders for awareness, control and vividness, with the LuCiD factor names shown as hints [C2][C35]. At onboarding and at intervals, the app asks the eight-point frequency question so that your progress can be compared with published norms rather than only with yourself [B49].
