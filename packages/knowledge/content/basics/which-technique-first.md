There are more than twenty techniques in this section, and the evidence for them is uneven. A systematic review of 35 studies judged the methodological quality "relatively low" and found that none of the techniques had been verified to work reliably and consistently, though some were promising, above all MILD combined with wake-back-to-bed [B34]. So the honest answer to "which first?" is the one with the best support and the lowest cost, in this order.

## 1. Recall and a journal, before any technique

Every training programme in the research makes dream recall the gate. The Lucidity Institute course will not start induction until recall has been steady for seven consecutive nights [D5], and in the large international induction study recall predicted success [D12]. If you recall fewer than one dream a night, start with the journal ([Journaling](/learn/techniques/journaling) and [Dream recall](/learn/dream-signs/dream-recall)) and come back here.

## 2. MILD with wake-back-to-bed

MILD, the mnemonic technique, is the best supported. In the Australian and international field studies it was linked to lucid dreams on roughly 17 to 20 percent of nights at home, and about 46 percent of attempts when people fell asleep within five minutes of finishing [B37][B38][B39]. Wake-back-to-bed is an amplifier rather than a technique of its own: an alarm after about 5 to 6 hours, a short time awake, then back to sleep with the intention. An alarm at 4.5 hours was too early, and an hour awake did better than 30 minutes [D13][D14].

## 3. Add one thing at a time

Once MILD with wake-back-to-bed is routine, there are two reasonable next steps. SSILD, the sensory cycling technique, has one large field study behind it, with lucid dreams on 16.9 percent of nights [B38]. Morning-nap WILD suits free days: it is a lab-documented phenomenon, but no controlled training trial has measured it [B15][D1]. Sound cues paired with pre-sleep training have been replicated in two laboratories and one app study [B42][B43][B44].

## What not to start with

- **Reality testing on its own.** It was the least effective arm in the induction studies and added nothing on top of MILD [B37][B38][B35]. It is still useful as part of the Tholey combined technique and for dream signs, just not as the only thing you do.
- **An eight-week mindfulness course.** Long-term meditators report more lucid dreams, but a standard eight-week course did not change the frequency [B46].
- **Forcing WILD.** Active effort works against it; see [Common mistakes and dry spells](/learn/basics/common-mistakes-and-dry-spells).

## If sleep suffers

Drop the alarms and keep only intention and reflection for a week. LaBerge's advice is to let go for a while, because lucid dreams sometimes appear after you stop trying so hard [D1]. The course in Train follows this order.
