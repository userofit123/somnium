Lucid dreaming collects myths easily, because it is rare enough to be mysterious and common enough that everyone has a friend who does it. These are the ones the research speaks to.

## "A lucid dream means you can control it"

Lucidity is knowing that you are dreaming. Control is a separate thing, and the standard questionnaire definition treats it as optional [B50]. In a Brazilian survey of 3,427 people, 77 percent had experienced lucidity but only 29 percent reported dream control [B31].

## "Almost nobody has them"

A meta-analysis of 50 years of studies found that 55 percent of people have had at least one lucid dream and 23 percent have them monthly or more [B32]. A representative German sample gave 51 percent for a lifetime [B33].

## "It is only a daydream, not proven"

In 1981 LaBerge and colleagues verified lucid dreams by having dreamers signal with agreed eye movements during REM sleep [A38]. In 2021 several laboratories showed that sleepers in lucid REM could answer questions by eye or facial signals [B27].

## "A tribe called the Senoi teach dream control to everyone"

The Senoi dream theory is a myth; Domhoff's study of the source material found it does not stand up [A31]. Carlos Castaneda's writing is fiction that was presented as ethnography [A33]. Both are covered in [Debunked stories](/learn/history/debunked-stories).

## "Reality checks alone will do it"

Reviews found reality testing on its own ineffective in most studies that included it [B35], and it added nothing to MILD in the field studies [B37][B38].

## "A brain stimulation device will do it for you"

The 2014 study of frontal 40 Hz stimulation reported lucid dreams on 77 percent of trials, but the replication found sham stimulation did as well [B23][B24].

## "Meditation courses make you lucid"

Long-term meditators report more lucid dreams, but an eight-week mindfulness course did not change the frequency [B46].

## "The harder you try, the sooner it works"

The advice from the people who teach it is the opposite: trying too hard is a stumbling block, and letting go for a few days sometimes brings lucid dreams on [D1].
