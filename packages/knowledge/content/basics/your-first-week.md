The first week of learning to lucid dream is not about lucid dreaming at all. It is about recall, because every programme in the research treats recall as the gate, and the plain way to build it is a journal beside the bed [D5][D12].

## What to do

- **Keep the journal where you can reach it.** Write the moment you wake, before checking a phone. Even "no dreams" is worth a line, because it keeps the habit going [D20].
- **Ask "what was I dreaming?" before you move.** Lie still for a minute and let the last scene come back, then write what you have, however small [D5].
- **Give yourself the chance.** Extra sleep in the morning gives more dream time, and the studies that used logbooks gave people a full week of nothing but logging first [D11][D12].
- **Do not start alarms or techniques yet.** The international study's first week was logbook only; techniques began in week two [D12].

## How you know the week worked

The goal is at least one dream recalled on seven nights in a row, and two or more a night is better, since recall predicted success in the large induction study [D5][D12]. That is also the rule the app's course uses before it moves you on.

## What to expect

Your recall will probably look as if it improves quickly. Part of that is real: Aspy showed that keeping a logbook enhances recall, and that single retrospective questions underestimate it [B48]. Part of it is only that you are now counting, so treat the first weeks as a new baseline rather than a result [B48].

If you want to try reality checks as well, there is no hurry. In the international study, one week of daytime reality testing gave no benefit [D12].

## Look after your sleep

Nothing in week one should cost you sleep. If it does, stop; LaBerge's rule throughout is to relax and let go for a few days if you are losing sleep over this [D1]. For the full order of steps after this week, see [Which technique should I try first?](/learn/basics/which-technique-first).
