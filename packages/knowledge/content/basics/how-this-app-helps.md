Somnium is a journal first and a trainer second. That order is deliberate: every training programme in the literature begins with dream recall, and the best-supported induction technique depends on it.

## Recall comes first

Dream recall predicts success. Lucid dream frequency correlates strongly with dream recall frequency in a representative sample (r = .57) [A60], and better general recall predicted induction success in the largest home study of techniques [B38]. Recall can be trained: keeping a logbook raises it, and single retrospective questions underestimate it [B48]. The Lucidity Institute's course will not let a student start induction until they have recalled at least one dream a night for seven nights running [D5].

The journal therefore makes capture fast, keeps the bedside instructions from the research close at hand, and tracks dreams per night from the entries rather than from a one-off question [B48].

## Signs are the cue

LaBerge's "dreamsigns" are the peculiar, recurring features of your own dreams that can serve as cues to recognise the dreaming state [C1]. His exercise is simple: collect at least a dozen dreams, mark the signs, classify each into inner awareness, action, form or context, count them, and target the most frequent category [C1]. The Lucidity Institute reported that people were more likely to reach lucidity if they showed a high awareness of the strangeness of their dreamsigns [C2].

The app does the counting for you. It reads each dream on the device, suggests signs and elements for you to confirm, ranks them by frequency and by how often they coincide with lucidity, and lets you rate each sign on the 0 to 4 awareness scale [C2]. Nothing leaves your device for this.

## Techniques with their evidence

The Train section teaches MILD, wake-back-to-bed, SSILD, reality testing and the rest, each with its origin, its evidence level and its known failure modes. MILD with wake-back-to-bed is the best-supported home protocol, producing lucid dreams on roughly 17 to 20 percent of nights in motivated samples after one week [B37][B38]. Reality testing alone did not work in either large study [B37][B38]. The app says so, rather than pretending every technique is equal.

The variables the studies found to matter are the ones the app logs each night: which techniques you used, how long you spent, and how quickly you fell back asleep. Falling asleep within ten minutes of finishing the technique roughly doubled the success rate in the international study [B38].

## Honest statistics

Dream series are small and autocorrelated. The app shows counts next to every percentage, suppresses comparisons under about 20 dreams, and prefers "nights with technique X: 8 lucid of 40" over a bare rate [C12a]. It compares your typical themes with published prevalence from a 1,181-student survey [C13] and your content with mid-century norms, with the caveat that those are student dreams from another era [C9].

## Safety built in

The cautionary literature is part of the app, not a footnote. Deliberate induction attempts correlate with sleep problems, stress and dissociation in one student sample [B71], and the main induction methods fragment sleep [B69]. The app recommends wake-back-to-bed on no more than a few nights a week, never shortens total sleep to make room for practice, and asks you to pause if lucid dreams become distressing or waking life starts to feel dreamlike [B69][B70][B71]. It does not recommend any substance [B52][B56].

## Private by design

Your dream text and every personal field are encrypted on your device before they are stored. The server holds only sealed data and never reads it. Analysis, including sign detection and theme matching, runs on the device. There is no analytics, no telemetry and no cloud model.

## Where to start

Read "What is lucid dreaming?" and "Dream recall", then keep the journal for a week. When you can recall a dream most nights, open Train and begin with reality testing and dream signs, then MILD with wake-back-to-bed. The Learn articles link to the matching exercise wherever one exists.
