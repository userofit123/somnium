## What this shows

The dreams you marked as a nightmare, a bad dream, a false awakening or sleep paralysis, in the period. A nightmare wakes you up; a bad dream is upsetting too, but you sleep on [C18]. **How your dreams felt** is the average of your own feeling ratings, from -2 to +2.

## How to read it

Counts from a journal run higher than people's guesses about their own nights, so a number here can look high without anything having changed [C18]. In the study that set them apart, frequent nightmares went with poorer well-being more than bad dreams did [C18].

About 1 person in 13 has had sleep paralysis at some point [B6]. Among habitual lucid dreamers, 41% had false awakenings at least monthly [B8], in a small self-selected web survey of 90 people. Your own feeling ratings tend to come out more positive than what others read into a dream's words [C21].

## What to do

Do a reality check on every awakening: it catches false awakenings [B8]. If nightmares come often, read [Nightmares](/learn/benefits/nightmares) and [False awakenings and sleep paralysis](/learn/safety/false-awakenings-and-sleep-paralysis).
