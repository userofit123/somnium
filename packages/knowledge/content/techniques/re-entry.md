## Origin

LaBerge's book and the Lucidity Institute course. The book: "Play dead. If you remain perfectly motionless upon waking from a lucid (or non-lucid) dream, and deeply relax your body, there is a good chance that REM sleep will reassert itself" [D1]. The course teaches it as "Re-entering the Dream State" (Unit 4, Exercise 4) and recommends it after premature awakenings [D5].

## Evidence level

Case reports. Alan Worsley, quoted by LaBerge: "Lie very still - don't move a muscle! Relax and wait. The dream will return. I've had dozens of lucid dreams in a row with this method" [D1]. The community form, DEILD or "chaining", has its own article; the evidence is the same.

## Steps

1. Notice that you have woken, ideally from a dream. Do not move, open your eyes, or reach for anything [D1].
2. Relax the body deeply. LaBerge's relaxation preliminaries apply: release head, neck, back, arms and legs, breathe slowly [D1].
3. Imagine yourself back in the dream you just left, engaging the senses: what you were seeing, hearing and touching [D5].
4. When the dream returns, test your state and stabilise before doing anything else [D1][D5].

## Why awakenings happen and how to prevent them

"Novice lucid dreamers often wake up the moment they become lucid" [D1]. The fix is in the stabilisation article: keep cool, engage the senses, move, spin when vision fades [D1][D5]. Re-entry is what you do when that fails.

## Waking at will

The reverse skill matters too. To wake deliberately, withdraw attention and participation: stop moving, close the dream eyes, fix the gaze on an uninteresting point (Tholey found that fixation blurs the scene and wakes you in four to twelve seconds), or lie down on a dream bed and go to sleep [D1][D5]. Beware of false awakenings when using these; test your state [D5]. LaBerge advises against escaping frightening dreams by waking; a good use of waking at will is to preserve recall of something important [D1].

## When

Immediately on any awakening from a dream in the last third of the night. The REM period you left is often still running [D1][D13].

## Common errors

Moving; opening the eyes; excitement; trying to re-enter a dream after a false awakening without testing first [D22][D5]. And the standing tension with the journal: write immediately, or lie still? On nights you practise re-entry, re-enter first and jot key words when the chain ends [D1].
