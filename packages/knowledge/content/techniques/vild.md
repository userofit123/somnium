## Origin

Peter Harrison, posting as Pedro on LD4all in "The BIG VILD topic"; the fullest description by the author is archived in the Wikibooks Lucid Dreaming appendix [D23]. The expansion of the acronym varies: "Visually Incubated" on LD4all, "Visual Induction" elsewhere [D23].

## Evidence level

Anecdotal. Harrison claimed a "100% success rate with me so far", disputed in the thread; there is no study [D23]. The mechanism is dream incubation of a scene in which you are prompted to reality check, which resembles LaBerge's intention technique, in which the planned dream action is itself a dream sign [D1].

## Steps, from Harrison

1. Design a fixed dream situation: a short scene, not a plot. Harrison's example is a bare dark-red room with a door; two friends urge you to do a reality-check "competition"; you do both checks (close one eye and try to see your nose; pinch the nose and try to breathe), conclude you are dreaming, the friends react happily, and you walk to the door. Fix every detail and rehearse it during the day [D23].
2. Bedtime: go to bed when tired (Harrison: about 11 pm); usual snack and toilet an hour before [D23].
3. Pre-sleep routine: dream journal and pen beside the bed; bedside lamp; read for about an hour, ideally about lucid dreaming, until the eyes are heavy, then push through ten more minutes; turn the lamp off without getting up; lie in a normal position and do not adjust it; progressive relaxation, tensing each muscle group from the toes up for about 30 seconds and releasing, ending with attention between the eyes and the mind emptying [D23].
4. Incubation: eyes closed, think through the scene repeatedly, restarting at the end; shift from thinking to visualising it in the first person; let unrelated thoughts go and return to the scene; keep every detail identical. At some point you shift into the dream without noticing; you then do the checks, become lucid and head for the door [D23].
5. After lucidity: Harrison adds shortcuts (if flying is hard, create a jetpack, then recognise that too is imagined) and prolongation by physical sensation: spinning, jumping, touching the ground, clapping while spinning, rubbing hands on surfaces [D23].

## When

Harrison's version is a bedtime practice, which puts it at odds with the timing evidence: the first REM period is short and far from sleep onset [B2]. A reasonable adaptation is to incubate the scene on a night awakening or before a morning nap instead [D4].

## Common errors

Letting the scene drift into a story; changing details between rehearsals; staying up past the point of drowsiness. Harrison's instruction is to keep the scene short and identical every time [D23].

## Related

Dream incubation in general, choosing a topic before sleep, has a controlled study behind it, described in the incubation article [D27]. VILD applies the same idea to a reality check.
