## Origin

Patricia Garfield reported in 1975 that she increased prolonged lucid dreams from zero to about three per week with self-suggestion. LaBerge used it for the first sixteen months of his dissertation record, averaging 5.4 lucid dreams per month. Tholey used it as a component of the combined technique [D1].

## Evidence level

Case and self-experiment. LaBerge found effortful intention (MILD) far more reliable than non-effortful suggestion: one of six laboratory nights with suggestion versus twenty of twenty-one with MILD [D1]. The Lucidity Institute course calls autosuggestion "a low-key, pressure-free method to use when you are feeling low on powers of mental concentration" [D5].

## Steps, as LaBerge gives them

1. Relax completely in bed, using progressive relaxation if needed [D1].
2. While deeply relaxed, suggest to yourself that you are going to have a lucid dream, tonight or soon. "Avoid putting intentional effort into your suggestion. Do not strongly insist with statements like 'Tonight I will have a lucid dream!'" Instead cultivate genuine expectation [D1].

Tholey recommends suggestions "immediately before sleep, while in a relaxed state", cautioning that "an effort of will must be avoided" [D1].

## When

At bedtime or on a night awakening, particularly on nights when you are too tired for MILD's visualisation, or during a dry spell when LaBerge's advice is to switch to a lower-effort method [D1][D5].

## Post-hypnotic suggestion

LaBerge tried post-hypnotic suggestion on three occasions and succeeded twice. He cites Joseph Dane's dissertation, in which fifteen women who had never had lucid dreams developed a personal dream symbol under hypnosis and then visualised it while asking for a lucid dream; they reported longer, more involving lucid dreams in the laboratory than hypnotised controls [D1]. That is one small controlled study, not replicated to the knowledge of this app's sources.

## Common errors

- Insisting or willing, which both LaBerge and Tholey say defeats the method [D1].
- Expecting MILD-level results; by LaBerge's own record it is a weaker method [D1].
- Dropping the relaxation step; the suggestion is meant to be given in a relaxed state [D1].

## Related: Morley's hypnagogic affirmation

Charlie Morley's "hypnagogic affirmation", falling asleep mentally reciting a phrase such as "When I dream, I know that I'm dreaming", is autosuggestion carried into sleep onset [D32]. The evidence level is the same: case and anecdotal.
