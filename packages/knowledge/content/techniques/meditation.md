## Origin

Every contemplative tradition that practises dream yoga makes daytime attention the base: Alan Wallace builds on shamatha, attentional stability [D31]; Charlie Morley on mindfulness of dream and sleep [D32]; Andrew Holecek on mindfulness as the gateway [D33]. In secular form, Tholey observed that his questioning habit matured into a "passively receptive focus on current experience" [D8], and the community's all-day awareness is the same idea [D20].

## Evidence level

A correlation and a negative trial. Baird and colleagues compared 38 long-term meditators with 140 meditation-naive adults: meditators reported 1.11 versus 0.45 lucid dreams per month, about two and a half times more, with no difference in dream recall; in meditators, lucidity tracked the observing and decentering facets of trait mindfulness. But randomising the naive group to an eight-week mindfulness-based stress reduction course, an active control or a waiting list produced no change in lucid-dream frequency at post-test or follow-up [B46]. So trait mindfulness correlates with lucidity; a standard course does not cause it, at least not in eight weeks [B46].

A survey of 528 people found lucid-dream frequency correlated with dispositional mindfulness, specifically mindful presence rather than acceptance, and only among people with meditation experience; causality is undetermined [D18].

## What to practise

Wallace's sequence [D31]:

1. Mindfulness of breathing, to settle the body and attention.
2. Settling the mind in its natural state: observing thoughts and images as they arise without following them.
3. Awareness of awareness: resting in the knowing itself.
4. Then the Western techniques: affirmations, planning and imagining outcomes, the reflective attitude, dream journals.
5. Then dream yoga proper: "emanation and transformation" and the illusory nature of experience.

The Lucidity Institute course includes a 20-minute concentration meditation in its third unit, alongside MILD, because MILD's visualisation needs sustained attention [D5].

## When

Daily, as a foundation. The honest expectation is that meditation improves the attention MILD needs and the calm that stabilisation needs, rather than producing lucid dreams by itself [B46][D5].

## Common errors

- Expecting an induction effect from meditation alone; the randomised trial found none [B46].
- Mixing meditation into SSILD, which its author lists as a failure mode [D19].
- Mistaking relaxation for attention. The hypnagogic imagery technique fails from "too much involvement and too little attention" [D1].

## A note on the traditions

Dream yoga and lucid dreaming share a core skill but differ in aim, and should not simply be equated [A14]. The traditions section presents the practices as the traditions describe them.
