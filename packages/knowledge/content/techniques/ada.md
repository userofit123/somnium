## Origin

Posted by the DreamViews member KingYoshi on 28 March 2011 as "All Day Awareness, A DILD Tutorial". He credits the basic concept to another member, Naiya, and says he coined the term and acronym [D20].

## Evidence level

Anecdotal (community). KingYoshi's own claim is 107 dream-initiated lucid dreams logged on the site, "hovering around the 22 lucid dreams per month mark", about 90 percent of them from awareness rather than a reality check; the thread collects many user success reports. There is no controlled study [D20]. The idea is close to Tholey's observation that the questioning habit matures into a "passively receptive focus on current experience" [D8], and to the finding that lucid-dream frequency correlates with mindful presence in people with meditation experience [D18]. Note that a randomised eight-week mindfulness course did not increase lucid dreams, so the correlation is not proof that daytime attention causes lucidity [B46].

## The practice, as KingYoshi describes it

1. ADA "is noticing and paying attention to the subtle things in life that most people ignore or take for granted": footsteps, the feel of shoes, muscles working, blinking, breathing, smells and whether they change, shadows of every structure, the keys under your fingers, the clothes on your body [D20].
2. "At first, you will have to force yourself to be completely aware of your surroundings"; with practice it becomes natural and "the dream itself becomes your dream sign" [D20].
3. Getting started, added after reader feedback: start small. Pick several times a day when you are not busy and spend 5 to 10 minutes noticing everything you can; do some reality checks in those sessions; increase session frequency as it gets easier. "Be sure not to overwork yourself. If you start feeling mentally exhausted, take a day off" [D20].
4. Reality checks are for confirming, not creating, lucidity. Before each check, become aware of your surroundings and "pretend that everything around you is actually a dream", then test [D20].
5. Dream signs still matter as recurring elements that build familiarity with the dream world [D20].
6. Journal every dream; lie still and recap before writing; write "Couldn't remember any dreams" if nothing comes. KingYoshi's recall aid: alarms after 4 and 6 hours, then hourly [D20].

## When

Throughout the day, built up from short sessions. The thread describes dry spells of one to two months broken by returning to daytime awareness together with MILD [D20].

## Common errors

From the thread: treating ADA as a mechanical "Am I dreaming?" check; trying to sustain it all day from the start, causing stress; neglecting the journal [D20].

## How the app supports it

The reminders can be set as short "notice everything" sessions rather than as checks, and the journal accepts "no recall" entries so that the habit is logged on empty mornings too. The app presents ADA's success rates as the author's reports, not as evidence.
