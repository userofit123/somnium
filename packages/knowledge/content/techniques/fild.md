## Origin

Posted by the DreamViews member Hargarts (often written Hargart) on 16 July 2004 as "Finger Induced Lucid Dream (FILD)", with "original directions from LD4ALL" appended, so the technique was first described on LD4all. It was previously also called HILD after its creator [D21].

## Evidence level

Anecdotal (community); no study [D21]. It belongs to the wake-initiated family, for which there is no controlled training trial [B15].

## Setup

1. Go to sleep normally; no hypnosis or visualisation [D21].
2. You must be extremely close to falling asleep. The best time is the middle of the night or the morning; morning attempts "can often cause shorter LDs" [D21].
3. Find a way to wake in the night: an alarm, intention, or waking from a dream [D21].
4. On waking, make FILD the first thing in mind; relax and return to sleep as normal. The goal is the point just before you fall asleep; if you fall asleep, try again on the next awakening [D21].

## Steps

1. Imagine playing a piano with the index and middle finger of one hand: press the right finger down while the left is up, then the left down and the right up; repeat [D21].
2. Press so lightly that the keys would not go down. "You should basically feel your muscles contract, but your fingers should hardly move" [D21].
3. When you reach the near-sleep point, start the movement and focus on it. "Do not count your finger movements or talk to yourself in your head. Also, do not try to make yourself go to sleep" [D21].
4. After about 10 to 20 seconds (the long version says 10 to 30), pinch your nose with the other hand and try to breathe in; if you can, you are dreaming [D21].
5. If the check fails, do not repeat every 30 seconds; relax for a few minutes and try once more. If it fails again, sleep and try on a later awakening [D21].

## Transition tip

If you wake when trying to get out of bed in the dream, keep the eyes closed, keep pinching the nose and breathing through it, wait 10 to 30 seconds, visualise your room until the image comes, then get up. Hargarts notes that the real-world finger movement transitions into the dream: you dream you are still moving your fingers [D21].

## When

On a night awakening after several hours of sleep, when you are already drowsy [D21]. Like every wake-initiated method, it depends on the long late REM periods [B2].

## Common errors

Starting while too awake; counting or narrating; lying there for 20 minutes moving the fingers; repeated nose checks that wake you [D21].

## A note on the nose-pinch test

The nose-pinch breathing check is one of the two tests Aspy used in his studies; it comes from community practice rather than from LaBerge [D11][D12]. No study of its individual reliability was located [C1].
