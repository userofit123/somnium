## Origin

Developed in 2011 by the author posting as Cosmic Iron on a Chinese lucid-dreaming forum, refined collaboratively, then published in English about a year later. The "official tutorial 2.0" is dated 24 January 2013 [D19][A59]. A specific Chinese forum name appearing in some accounts could not be confirmed [A58].

## Evidence level

Anecdotal (community) until the International Lucid Dream Induction Study (2020), where SSILD with wake-back-to-bed produced lucid dreams on 16.9 percent of nights, statistically equivalent to MILD; a MILD/SSILD hybrid did worse than either [D12]. This makes SSILD the only forum-born technique with a controlled field study behind it [D12]. Because it rests on one study, Tan and Fan rate it "insufficient/ambiguous" [B35].

## The cycle

Three steps, eyes closed [D19]:

- Sight: attend to the darkness behind the eyelids; do not strain the eye muscles or move the eyes to look for visuals.
- Hearing: relax the eyes further and attend to the ears: internal sounds (ringing, heartbeat) or external sounds if quiet.
- Touch: attend to the body for tingling, heaviness, lightness or spinning; if nothing, notice the blanket's weight, the heartbeat, the air temperature.

## Steps, official tutorial 2.0

1. Go to bed early, preferably before 11 pm; set an alarm for 4 to 5 hours later [D19].
2. Get up for about 5 to 10 minutes: bathroom, rinse the mouth, walk or stretch, without becoming fully awake [D19].
3. Return to bed in a comfortable position, preferably different from your usual one; if you normally struggle to sleep, use the usual position [D19].
4. Fast cycles: 4 to 6 cycles, only a few seconds per step, as a warm-up [D19].
5. Slow cycles: 3 to 4 cycles, at least about 30 seconds per step, timing flexible, do not count. "This step is the most important one" [D19].
6. Return to your most comfortable position and fall asleep as quickly as possible. "Don't overthink whether it will work" [D19].

In the 2020 study the instructions were simplified to four fast cycles (2 to 3 seconds per step) then four to six slow cycles (about 20 seconds per step), with at least four slow cycles required [D12].

## What happens next

The author calls SSILD "a hybrid", neither WILD nor DILD. The repeated sensory focus "conditions the mind and body into a subtle state that is optimized for lucid dreams to occur naturally". Possible outcomes: hypnagogia during the cycles or on re-awakening (stay passive; "it is better to expect NOTHING at all will happen"); very realistic false awakenings; a dream-initiated lucid dream in which you notice something odd; or a genuine awakening, in which case relax the head into the pillow and do a few medium-paced cycles [D19]. In 2011, he says, "nobody knew why it worked" [D19].

## False awakenings

"Perform a reality check after every awakening following SSILD", including awakenings that feel like insomnia; some users lost sleep doing cycles inside a dream [D19].

## When

After 4 to 5 hours of sleep, never at bedtime, which the author calls "ineffective because early sleep is mostly NREM" [D19].

## Common errors, the author's list

Trying too hard and expecting to see, hear or feel things; straining the eyes; becoming excited or discouraged; mixing in breathing, meditation or relaxation techniques; practising at bedtime; losing sleep; itching (scratch, then do an extra cycle); insomnia (reduce the hours slept before the alarm or the time spent awake) [D19].
