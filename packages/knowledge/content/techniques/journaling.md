## Origin

Hervey de Saint-Denys kept a dream journal from his early teens and built the first book on directing dreams from it [A17][C5]. LaBerge's chapter on recall and the Lucidity Institute course set out the modern bedside routine [D1][D5].

## Evidence level

A prerequisite with support for its effect on recall, and no trial showing journaling alone induces lucidity [B48][B33]. Lucid-dream frequency correlates with dream-recall frequency (r = .57 in a representative sample) [B33], and better recall predicted induction success in the international study [B38]. Keeping a logbook enhances recall, most in people whose recall was improving [B48]. Schredl found a diary increased recall in low and medium recallers but decreased it in high recallers [C30]. The Lucidity Institute course will not let you start induction until you have recalled at least one dream a night for seven consecutive nights [D5].

## Steps: the bedside routine

1. Before bed, write the date and "I will remember my dreams" [D1][D5].
2. Keep the journal by the bed with a pen, light and clock [C2].
3. On any awakening, before moving or thinking of anything else, ask "What was I dreaming?" and hold the question. "Don't move from the position in which you awaken, as any body movement may make your dream harder to remember" [C1].
4. If nothing comes, ask "What was I just thinking?" and "How was I just feeling?", then rebuild the dream backwards from any fragment: "ask yourself what happened before that, and before that" [C1].
5. Write immediately: at least a few key words, the time, key events, feelings and "any verbatim speech or text". Expand in the morning [C1][C2].
6. Give each dream "a short, catchy title" [C1].
7. Record fragments "no matter how unimportant", moods, and "no recall" [C1][D20].

## What to write

LaBerge asks for how images "look and sound and smell" and "the way you felt in the dream - emotional reactions are important clues"; anything unusual; sketches [C1]. The course logs, per night, bedtime, the time of each awakening, the number of dreams, the number of lucid dreams and which recall aids were used [C2]. The research adds a self-rated emotion per dream, because text-based reading of emotion under-counts positive feeling [C21].

## Recall aids

- Extra sleep: "It is more effective for increasing dream recall to sleep later in the morning than to go to bed earlier at night", because REM periods lengthen later in the night [C1][C2].
- A visible reminder by the bed [C2].
- An alarm at REM-rich times: "4.5, 6 and 7.5 hours after you go to sleep" [C1][C2].

## When

Every morning, starting now, and continuing through every later technique. Recall targets: at least one dream per night before induction techniques; the course's long-term goal is two or more per night, counting any fragment as one [D5][C2].

## Common errors

- Waiting until you get up. "Don't wait until you get up in the morning ... be sure to write down at least a few key words" [C1].
- Thinking of the day's concerns first [C1].
- Skipping empty mornings; "Couldn't remember any dreams" is an entry [D20].
- Measuring progress by a one-off question; the app counts dreams per morning from the entries and warns that the first journal weeks inflate apparent improvement [B48].
