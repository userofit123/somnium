## Origin

Paul Tholey, Frankfurt, first used the reflection technique in 1959 and published it in English in "Techniques for inducing and manipulating lucid dreams" (1983), based on "research conducted over several years and with over 200 subjects" [D6]. He described it in his own words in Lucidity Letter in 1988 and 1989 [D7][D8].

## Evidence level

Tholey's own multi-subject studies; later independent tests found it less effective than MILD. Aspy cites a 2018 study that found lucid dreams on at most 6 percent of days with the combined technique [D12]. Tholey claimed that subjects who had never had a lucid dream "will have the first one after a median time of 4 to 5 [weeks]" and that most who persist "experience at least one lucid dream every night"; treat this as an originator's claim [D1][D8]. The 2024 Russian-language review groups it with SSILD and DEILD as "promising, requiring further research" [B35].

## The reflection technique in Tholey's words

"The subject asks him or herself several times during the course of the day: 'Am I awake, or am I dreaming?' The purpose is to achieve a generally critical attitude towards one's state of consciousness" [D8]. Tholey had his first lucid dream after four weeks [D8].

Three factors he found decisive: ask the question (1) as often as possible, (2) as much as possible in situations similar to dream experiences, and (3) ideally just before falling asleep, especially in the early morning hours. "The experienced lucid dreamer does not need to ask the critical question during the day" [D7].

## The combined technique

Tholey expanded the reflection technique "by incorporating elements of intention and auto-suggestion" [D8]. The complete step list from the 1983 paper was not available in full text. LaBerge's published adaptation, explicitly marked "Adapted from Tholey", is the Reflection-Intention technique [D1]:

1. Plan when you intend to test your state: a dozen or more times a day at chosen occasions, plus whenever anything is surprising, odd, emotionally strong, or your memory feels unresponsive. Use imagery to remember to ask the question [D1].
2. Test your state: ask "Am I dreaming or awake?", look for oddities, think back over the last few minutes, read some text twice. Do not conclude you are awake without solid proof [D1].
3. Imagine yourself dreaming: having satisfied yourself you are awake, say "Okay, I'm not dreaming, now. But if I were, what would it be like?" Imagine as vividly as possible that everything you perceive is a dream; look for your target dream signs; then say "The next time I'm dreaming, I will remember to recognize that I'm dreaming" [D1].
4. Imagine doing what you intend to do in your lucid dream: decide in advance on an action, continue the fantasy and see yourself carrying it out after becoming lucid. Firmly resolve to recognise that you are dreaming and do the intended action [D1].

Tholey's autosuggestion component is given "immediately before sleep, while in a relaxed state", with the caution that "an effort of will must be avoided" [D1].

## Tholey's tests

Pair the question with a real test. Physical: turn 180 degrees and try to stand still; in a dream "the body continues to turn ... or the surroundings begin to revolve". Mental: "trying to remember what has happened during the immediately preceding period of time". Because the memory test fails after a false awakening, turn a light on when you wake; "if the light does not go on, this may signify a dream state" [C4].

## When

Daily, for weeks. Tholey's own figure is a median of four to five weeks to the first lucid dream with daily practice [D1][D6]. The Lucidity Institute course assigns eight occasions a day and asks students to log hits [D5].

## Common errors

- Asking without testing, or testing without a genuine question [D8].
- Expecting a one-week result; the published tests of short duration found little [D12].
- Forgetting the intention and autosuggestion components, which turn a daytime habit into a pre-sleep resolve [D8].
- Using effortful willing in the autosuggestion step, which Tholey says to avoid [D1].
