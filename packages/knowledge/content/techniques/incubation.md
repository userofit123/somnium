## Origin

Deirdre Barrett's 1993 study "The 'Committee of Sleep'", in Dreaming, using William Dement's 1974 incubation instructions [D27]. LaBerge's "lucid dream incubation" adds the intention to become lucid [D1].

## Evidence level

A controlled study for ordinary incubation: students incubated a self-chosen problem nightly for a week; about two-thirds had dreams addressing the problem and about one-third reached a solution in a dream, some rated by outside judges as objectively solving it [D27]. The lucid extension is LaBerge's self-report [D1]. On creativity more broadly, no experiment has shown that cultivating lucidity increases waking creativity, though lucidity correlates with creativity in cross-sectional work [B82].

## Steps: Barrett's incubation

1. Write the problem as a brief phrase and keep the notepad by the bed [D27].
2. Review it for a few minutes before bed [D27].
3. Once in bed, visualise the problem as a concrete image and let it be the last thing in mind [D27].
4. Optionally place an object representing the problem on the bedside table [D27].
5. On waking, lie still, do not jump up, and note any emotion if the dream does not come immediately [D27].

## Steps: LaBerge's lucid dream incubation

1. Formulate a single phrase describing the topic and write it down [D1].
2. Resolve to become lucid and carry it out [D1].
3. In the dream, once lucid, pursue the topic; "spin to your target" if the dream is fading, repeating the phrase while spinning [D1].

## Related practices

- The intention technique, adapted from Tholey, has you resolve to do a chosen action in the dream, ideally one that is itself a dream sign such as flying. Dreamers sometimes do the action before becoming lucid and then realise "This is what I wanted to do in my dream. Therefore, I must be dreaming!" [D1].
- VILD applies incubation to a reality-check scene [D23].
- The Tibetan "power of resolution": by day think that "all things are of the substance of dreams"; at night "firmly resolve" to comprehend the dream state [D1][D35].

## When

At bedtime for Barrett's method, since it needs no particular REM timing; on a night awakening for the lucid version, so that the intention is fresh when the long REM periods arrive [D1][B2].

## Common errors

- Choosing a vague topic; both methods want a single phrase or a concrete image [D27][D1].
- Jumping up on waking before the dream is recalled [D27].
- Expecting solutions to arrive as answers; Barrett's dreams "addressed" the problem far more often than they solved it [D27].
