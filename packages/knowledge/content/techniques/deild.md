## Origin

The practice of lying still after a dream to re-enter it is in LaBerge's book: "Play dead. If you remain perfectly motionless upon waking from a lucid (or non-lucid) dream, and deeply relax your body, there is a good chance that REM sleep will reassert itself." LaBerge quotes Alan Worsley, the first person to signal from a lucid dream in a laboratory: "Lie very still - don't move a muscle! Relax and wait. The dream will return. I've had dozens of lucid dreams in a row with this method" [D1]. The acronym DEILD and the word "chaining" come from DreamViews; a post by Klace, reproduced on LD4all on 5 March 2007, presents DEILD as "a substitute for WILD" [D22].

## Evidence level

Case reports (Worsley, LaBerge) and anecdotal community claims. Klace claimed a "100% success rate if done correctly", which thread replies disputed [D22]. The 2024 Russian-language review lists dream-exit and re-entry among techniques "promising, requiring further research" [B35].

## Steps, from Klace, paraphrased

1. Train yourself to notice that you wake after each dream [D22].
2. On waking, keep the eyes closed and stay completely still; the REM period continues. You may enter sleep paralysis, which is natural and harmless [D22].
3. Stay still; lucidity follows shortly; do what you wish [D22].
4. To chain: when the dream fades, stay completely still as you wake and repeat [D22].

Community guides add: imagine yourself back in the dream, then stabilise and reality test on arrival; best after four to six hours of sleep [D22].

## Why it can work

Waking from a dream usually means waking from REM. If you stay still, the REM period often continues and the dream returns [D1]. This is the same fact that makes waking from a dream the best time for MILD [D13].

## When

On any awakening from a dream, especially in the last third of the night. It pairs naturally with the dream re-entry exercise, which is the version taught in the Lucidity Institute course [D5].

## Common errors

Moving to check a clock or write; opening the eyes; excitement on waking; mistaking a false awakening for a real one, so always test [D22]. The usual tension with journaling is real: the journal wants you to write immediately, DEILD wants you still. A compromise is to re-enter first on nights you are practising, and to jot key words only after the chain ends [D1].
