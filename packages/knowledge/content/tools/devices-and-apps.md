Devices promise to do the noticing for you: detect REM, deliver a cue, and let the cue remind you that you are dreaming. The idea is sound. The products mostly are not.

## The DreamLight and NovaDreamer

The DreamLight, a mask that detected REM and flashed light cues, was developed by LaBerge and Levitan in the late 1980s [A42][A43]. Its validation study alternated cue and no-cue nights without the sleeper's knowledge: 11 subjects reported 32 lucid dreams, 22 on cue nights and 10 on non-cue nights [B41]. The light was woven into the dream 80 percent of the time, so daytime training to recognise it was essential [D1]. At home, the DreamLight was about as effective as MILD and the combination gave "five times as many lucid dreams" as no technique [D1].

The cheaper NovaDreamer, designed by Craig Webb, was released in 1993 and discontinued around 2004 [A42][A43]. No current consumer mask has comparable published validation [B41].

## Hearne's dream machine

Keith Hearne patented a "dream machine" that detected REM from breathing and delivered a cue, with UK and US patents granted in 1983 [A34]. It predates the DreamLight but was never validated in the same way.

## Sound cues and the one tested app

Targeted lucidity reactivation pairs a sound with a lucid mindset before sleep and replays it in REM. In a laboratory nap, 50 percent of participants had a signal-verified lucid dream [B42]; ring tones paired with reality testing gave 2 of 12 verified [B44]. Konkoly and colleagues ported the method to an Android app: lucid dreams rose from 0.74 to 2.11 per week in a 19-person experiment, and in a 120-person experiment trained cues held the rate while untrained or no cues let it decline. The cues produced measurable sleep disruption, and expectancy is an alternative explanation [B43]. This is the only peer-reviewed evidence for any lucid-dreaming app; commercial app claims beyond it are unverified [B43].

## Electrical stimulation and "40 Hz"

Voss and colleagues reported in 2014 that frontal alternating current at 40 Hz during REM produced lucid dreams on 77 percent of trials [B23]. "Lucid" was defined by rating-scale thresholds rather than eye signals, and participants had no induction training [B15]. The replication attempt by Blanchette-Carriere and colleagues found lucid dreams under stimulation and under sham alike, with no stimulation effect [B24]. A direct-current study found only weak self-rated increases confined to frequent lucid dreamers [B25]. No electrical stimulation protocol is established as an inducer, and consumer "40 Hz" devices rest on the unreplicated result [B15][B24].

## The crowdfunded masks

The Remee, a timer-based LED mask with no REM sensing, raised over half a million dollars on Kickstarter in 2012. The Aurora headband by iWinks, which used EEG, raised about 239,000 dollars in late 2013, shipped late and was later abandoned when its app was withdrawn [A55]. A 2019 review in Frontiers in Neuroscience concluded that portable induction devices were of unproven reliability [B56].

## REMspace

In September 2024 the company REMspace, led by Michael Raduga, issued press releases claiming the first "two-way communication" between two sleeping people in lucid dreams, followed by claims of controlling a car from a dream. These results have not been peer-reviewed. Konkoly has publicly criticised the company for releasing "unfinished, undocumented projects inaccurately represented as breakthroughs", and Raduga's 2023 self-implanted brain electrode is widely noted as a safety concern. These claims should be labelled as unverified marketing [A57].

## What this app does

The app offers an optional sound cue with the daytime training step the research requires, off by default and limited to a window you set, and it records whether the cue appeared in your dreams [B42][B43]. It does not detect REM, does not stimulate anything, and does not claim a success rate beyond the published studies. Reality-check reminders are a habit trainer, not an inducer: daily check counts did not predict lucid dreams in the international study [B38].
