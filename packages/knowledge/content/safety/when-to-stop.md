Lucid-dream practice is optional. Here is how to tell when to put it down for a while, and what the research says happens when you do.

## The signs

Pause the practice and talk to a professional if [B71][B70]:

- lucid dreams become distressing;
- waking reality starts to feel dreamlike;
- you notice increasing detachment from yourself or your surroundings.

Stop wake-back-to-bed and naps if daytime sleepiness, mood or concentration suffer for more than a few days [B69].

## Why these signs

Over two months in one student sample, the frequency of induced lucid dreams predicted increases in dissociation and schizotypy, controlling for baseline; spontaneous lucid dreams did not [B71]. The effects were small, but they point the same way as the clinical concern that practice could "further empower deliria and hallucinations" in vulnerable people [B72]. Detachment and a dreamlike waking life are the early signs to watch [B70][B71].

Sleep is the other reason. The main methods fragment it [B69]. Fragmented sleep triggers sleep paralysis [B6], and most negative lucid-dreaming experiences follow failed or forced attempts [B76].

## Letting go is a technique

LaBerge: "One of the greatest stumbling blocks in learning almost any skill is trying too hard ... If you find you are losing sleep while struggling to have lucid dreams without result, let go of your efforts for a while. Relax and forget about lucid dreaming for a few days or a few weeks. Sometimes you will find that after you let go, lucid dreams will appear" [D1].

The literature treats dry spells as expected. LaBerge's remedies are rest, renewed motivation, and switching to a lower-effort method such as autosuggestion when concentration is low [D1][D5]. Daniel Love's CAT prescribes refreshing its first stage every few months [D24]. Community reports describe dry spells of one to two months broken by returning to daytime awareness together with MILD, which is anecdotal [D20].

## What to keep doing

The journal is safe to continue. It raises recall, it costs no sleep, and nothing in the cautionary literature concerns writing down dreams [B48]. Reality testing is a daytime habit with no sleep cost [B37]. What to drop first is anything that interrupts sleep: wake-back-to-bed, naps, sound cues, and the wake-initiated methods that pass through sleep paralysis [B69][B43][B15].

## How the app helps you stop

Every induction feature can be switched off in Settings, together or one at a time, and the reminders stop with them. The journal keeps working. When you come back, the app's progression starts again from recall, not from where you left off, because recall is the gate every programme uses [D5].

## If you are worried

If any of the signs above apply, or if you have one of the conditions listed under "Who should be careful", the right next step is a conversation with a doctor or therapist, not another technique [B70][B72].
