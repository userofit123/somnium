Every effective induction method in this app is timed to the long REM periods late in the night, and the most effective ones interrupt sleep to get there [B2][B39]. That makes sleep the thing to protect.

## The rules the app displays

1. Sleep comes first. Never shorten total sleep to make room for wake-back-to-bed; schedule it on nights when you can sleep in afterwards [B69].
2. Use wake-back-to-bed at most two or three nights a week, and stop if daytime sleepiness, mood or concentration suffer for more than a few days [B69].
3. Keep the wake period to 30 to 60 minutes of quiet dream work, not screens or stimulation [B39].
4. If an induction attempt fails, let it go; most negative experiences come from forcing it [B76].
5. Do a reality check on every awakening; it catches false awakenings [B8].

## Why timing beats effort

Erlacher and Stumbrys found that an hour of dream work and MILD after about six hours of sleep gave self-rated lucid dreams in over half of participants; waking after 4.5 hours in a follow-up gave much lower rates [B39][B40]. The Lucidity Institute's nap studies found that waking an hour earlier and napping later worked better than anything done at bedtime [D4]. Practising harder at the wrong time is the common mistake; practising briefly at the right time is the evidence-based one.

## What LaBerge said

"Lucid dreaming, at least at first, requires good sleep and mental energy for concentration"; if you cannot allot more time to sleep, "it's probably not a good idea for you to work on lucid dreaming right now" [D1]. And: "If you find you are losing sleep while struggling to have lucid dreams without result, let go of your efforts for a while. Relax and forget about lucid dreaming for a few days or a few weeks. Sometimes you will find that after you let go, lucid dreams will appear" [D1].

## What the techniques' own authors said

SSILD's author lists "losing sleep" and "insomnia" as failure modes and prescribes less time asleep before the alarm or less time awake [D19]. The ADA tutorial says to take a day off when mentally exhausted [D20]. The international induction study told participants with insomnia to keep lights off, use a quieter alarm and spend less time awake [D12].

## Does the practice harm sleep?

The induction studies that measured it found no deterioration in sleep quality when lucidity was achieved, and sleep quality was slightly higher on lucid nights [B38][B65][B68]. The cross-sectional links between frequent lucid dreaming and poorer sleep are best explained by fragmented sleep causing lucidity rather than the reverse [B77][B69]. Sound cues during sleep, however, produced measurable disruption in the one app study [B43].

## The rest rule

The app's progression keeps wake-back-to-bed and naps to no more than three or four nights a week unless sleep remains good, and drops to intention and reflection only for a week if sleep suffers [D1]. The Insights screen shows your hours slept next to your lucid rate, so the trade-off is visible rather than hidden.
